About the project

Why SoughtOut Beyond?

The name comes from this verse. To be sought out is to be looked for and not forgotten. These machines were left behind, but they are not forsaken. SoughtOut Beyond goes looking for them and tells their stories.

The catalog

16 objects
LUN-001● Still working
Laser Ranging Retroreflector
A mirror on the Moon that scientists still shoot lasers at today.
“I have no batteries, no motors, and no computer. I just sit here and bounce light back to Earth.”
What it discoveredThe Moon drifts about 3.8 cm farther from Earth every year, and it has a fluid core.
Apollo 11 · Arrived Jul 20, 1969Tell me about it →
LUN-002● Retired
Lunar Roving Vehicle
The first car ever driven on another world.
“They folded me up like a lawn chair and drove me 27.8 kilometers across the Moon.”
What it discoveredIt carried astronauts to the Genesis Rock and helped bring home 76 kg of Moon rocks.
Apollo 15 · Arrived Jul 30, 1971Tell me about it →
LUN-003● Visited
Surveyor 3
The only robot on the Moon that astronauts came back to visit.
“I bounced twice before I settled. Two and a half years later, two astronauts walked over and took my camera home.”
What it discoveredMoon soil is like wet sand, strong enough to hold up an Apollo lander. It also sent back 6,326 TV pictures.
Surveyor · Arrived Apr 20, 1967Tell me about it →
MRS-001● Silent
Opportunity
Built to last 90 days. It kept going for almost 15 years.
“My job was 90 days and about one kilometer. I drove 45.”
What it discoveredHematite, a mineral that proves liquid water once existed on Mars.
Mars Exploration Rover · Arrived Jan 24, 2004Tell me about it →
MRS-002● Retired
Ingenuity Helicopter
The first aircraft to fly on another planet. Named by a high school student.
“I was supposed to fly five times. I flew 72.”
What it discoveredPowered, controlled flight works on another planet. It flew 17 km in 128.8 minutes and climbed as high as 24 m.
Mars 2020 · Arrived Feb 18, 2021Tell me about it →
MRS-003● Retired
InSight Lander
The lander that listened to the heartbeat of Mars.
“I never moved an inch. I just listened. I heard Mars shake 1,319 times.”
What it discoveredMars has a large liquid core and a thin, three-layered crust. Its mantle is full of giant lumps left by ancient impacts.
InSight · Arrived Nov 26, 2018Tell me about it →
DSP-001● Retired
Kepler Space Telescope
NASA’s first planet hunter. It found planets are everywhere.
“I stared at the same patch of sky, watching 150,000 stars for tiny blinks. Each blink was a planet passing in front.”
What it discoveredMore than 2,600 planets around other stars, and proof our galaxy has more planets than stars.
Kepler / K2 · Launched Mar 6, 2009Tell me about it →
LUN-004● Retired
Eagle Descent Stage
The bottom half of the first crewed Moon lander, left in the Sea of Tranquility.
“I held my crew 1.5 meters above the dust for 21 hours. When they left, I was their launch pad.”
What it discoveredPeople could land on the Moon and launch home. The crew brought back 21.55 kg of Moon rock.
Apollo 11 · Arrived Jul 20, 1969Tell me about it →
LUN-005● Retired
Falcon & ALSEP Station
Falcon’s descent stage, plus a science station that kept working for six years after the astronauts left.
“My crew drove 27.9 km in their rover. When they flew home, my science station kept listening until 1977.”
What it discoveredMoonquakes, the Moon’s magnetic field, and heat leaking out of its interior. The crew also brought back 77.31 kg of samples.
Apollo 15 · Arrived Jul 30, 1971Tell me about it →
MRS-004● Retired
Sojourner Rover
The first rover on Mars. It weighed 10.6 kg and stood just 30 cm tall.
“We landed inside giant airbags and bounced about 15 times. Then I rolled down a ramp onto Mars.”
What it discoveredRounded pebbles that hint Mars was once warm and wet. The mission sent home more than 17,000 images.
Mars Pathfinder · Arrived Jul 4, 1997Tell me about it →
MRS-005● Silent
Spirit
Opportunity’s identical twin. It landed in Gusev Crater, a place that may once have been a lake.
“My twin and I landed on opposite sides of Mars. I drove 7.7 km before my mission ended in 2010.”
What it discoveredWith Opportunity, proof from the rocks that water lasted on Mars’ surface long ago.
Mars Exploration Rover · Arrived Jan 3, 2004Tell me about it →
MRS-006● Retired
Viking 1 & 2 Landers
Viking 1 made the first truly successful landing on Mars and took the first photo from its surface.
“Minutes after landing, I took the first picture ever made on Mars. I kept sending weather reports for six years.”
What it discoveredSoil full of sulfur, unlike anything on Earth or the Moon. The two landers sent back 4,500 photos.
Viking · Arrived Jul 20, 1976Tell me about it →
MRS-007● Retired
Phoenix Lander
Landed farther north on Mars than any mission before it, as far north as northern Alaska is on Earth.
“I dug trenches, baked soil in tiny ovens, and watched bright clumps in my scoop vanish into the air. That was ice.”
What it discoveredWater ice just under the surface, and perchlorate, a chemical that could power microbes or help future astronauts.
Phoenix · Arrived May 25, 2008Tell me about it →
DSP-002● Retired
Dawn Spacecraft
NASA’s first truly interplanetary spaceship. It orbited two worlds in the asteroid belt: Vesta and Ceres.
“I ran out of fuel, so now I just circle Ceres. I’m its tiny new moon.”
What it discoveredCeres may once have had enough inner heat to make it a possible home for life.
Dawn · Launched Sep 27, 2007Tell me about it →
DSP-003● Retired
NEAR Shoemaker
The first spacecraft to orbit an asteroid, and the first to land on one.
“Nobody built me to land. I touched down on Eros at 6.4 km/h and kept talking for two more weeks.”
What it discoveredEros has no magnetic field. It sent home 10 times more data than planned, including 160,000 images.
NEAR · Arrived Feb 14, 2000Tell me about it →
DSP-004● Drifting
Pioneer 10 & 11
Pioneer 10 was the first spacecraft to cross the asteroid belt and fly past Jupiter.
“I was built for 21 months. I lasted more than 30 years. Now I’m drifting toward the star Aldebaran, carrying a map home.”
What it discoveredThe first close-up pictures of Jupiter, including its Great Red Spot, and of its moon Ganymede.
Pioneer · Launched Mar 2, 1972Tell me about it →
